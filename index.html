RV Storage Checklist: Preparing Your Rig for Off-Season Storage

Taking your motorhome, travel trailer, or fifth wheel across the Lone Star State is one of the best ways to explore the outdoors. However, when the road trips wind down and it is time to park your rig for the off-season, proper preparation is essential—especially for RV owners living along the Texas Gulf Coast.

Between intense summer heat, salty coastal air, and sudden tropical downpours, leaving an RV exposed in a driveway can quickly lead to roof leaks, faded paint, flat-spotted tires, and degraded interior trim. Following a comprehensive off-season prep checklist keeps your camper in peak condition and ready for your next coastal getaway.

Exterior Prep: Shielding Your RV from Coastal Air

Salt air and moisture carry unique corrosion risks for RV exteriors. Before parking your rig for an extended period, give the outside full attention:

  • Wash and Wax the Body: Thoroughly wash off road grime, insect residue, and coastal salt spray. Apply a high-quality marine or RV wax to protect the gel coat and graphics from harsh UV breakdown.
  • Inspect Roof Seals and Caulking: Check all roof seams, skylights, vents, and slide-out seals. Repair any cracking or peeling caulk with lap sealant to prevent water intrusion during heavy rains.
  • Clean and Extend Awnings to Dry: Scrub down vinyl and fabric awnings, letting them dry completely before rolling them up tight to prevent mold and mildew growth.
  • Protect Tires from Flat-Spotting: Inflate tires to the manufacturer's recommended PSI. If parking on dirt or gravel, set tires on rubber mats or wood blocks to prevent moisture absorption, and use UV-blocking tire covers.

Interior & Plumbing Prep: Humidity and System Care

High humidity along the bay can create interior moisture issues if an RV sits sealed up tight for months. Protecting your living space requires both plumbing maintenance and interior prep:

  • Drain All Water Tanks: Flush and drain your fresh water tank, grey tank, and black tank completely. Run fresh water through the lines to clear out sediment, and bypass the water heater before draining it.
  • Clean Out the Refrigerator and Pantry: Remove all perishable food, open the refrigerator and freezer doors, and prop them open to prevent lingering odors and mold.
  • Manage Interior Moisture: Place moisture absorbers or dehumifier buckets throughout the cabin to trap airborne moisture and keep upholstery smelling fresh.
  • Shut Off LP Gas and Electric: Turn off propane tanks at the valve and disconnect battery cables (or switch off the main isolation switch) to prevent phantom power draws from discharging your house batteries.
